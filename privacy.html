The short version

Rewind scans your camera roll on your phone to work out which days were a trip. That scan reads dates and locations already stored in your photos, and its results stay on the device.

A photo or video is uploaded only when you add it to a trip — and adding is always something you do: accepting a trip Rewind found, which uploads every photo it matched to that trip after telling you how many and asking what quality; picking photos for a trip yourself; or sharing a picture into Rewind and choosing a trip. Photos in trips you dismiss, and photos that belong to no trip, never leave the phone. A trip is private unless you decide otherwise: you can keep it to its members, open it to your friends, or make it public, and the same choice exists for your profile and your collections.

There is no advertising in Rewind, no analytics, and no tracking across apps or websites. We do not sell your data, and we do not share it for advertising. If the app crashes, a report of the crash is sent so the fault can be fixed; it carries no personal data.

What never leaves your phone

  • Your camera roll. Rewind reads your photo library to detect trips. The photos themselves, and the metadata of photos you never add to a trip, are never uploaded and never seen by us. Accepting a trip Rewind found is adding its photos, all of them, so the ones that stay are the ones in trips you dismiss and the ones that belong to no trip.
  • The scan. Working out which days were a trip, which photos belong to one, and which photos are "home", happens on the device. Nothing is uploaded by the scan itself, and the count it shows you ("we found 38 photos from this trip") is counted on the phone.
  • Songs and voice recordings. A song you pick for a film, or a voice note you record for one, stays on your phone. We store the song's title and artist and the timing of its beats so the film can be cut to it; the recording itself is never uploaded, and a film that carries it is cut on your phone.
  • Stories you export. A story image is composed and captured on the device and never touches a server.
  • Pictures handed to Rewind by another app. Something you share into Rewind from Photos or Safari waits on the device until you add it to a trip. If you never do, it is discarded.
  • Cameras on your network. Importing from an Insta360 camera happens over your local network or the camera's own hotspot. Nothing about that connection leaves your phone.

Location

Rewind's location data comes almost entirely from the coordinates already written into your photos by the camera that took them. The app also asks for your device's location so it can centre the map on you and, when you ask it to, place a photo that carries no location of its own. Those readings are used for the moment you asked and are not stored, except as the position written to that photo.

The home location is the one exception, and it is approximate by design: it is rounded to a grid of about five kilometres before it is ever written, and people who can see your profile can see it, because it is where your films start from.

Rewind does not track your location in the background. It has no background location capability at all; it cannot see where you are when it is closed.

Payments

Subscriptions and one-off purchases are sold by Apple, not by us. We never see your card details. Apple tells our payments provider that a purchase happened, and our provider tells our server which plan you are on — that is the whole of what we learn.

Health and fitness data

If you connect Apple Health, Rewind reads workouts and their GPS routes, and nothing else. It does not read heart rate, cadence, steps, weight, sleep, or any other health sample type. It does not write anything to Apple Health.

It reads only the dates of a trip you choose, and you select which workouts are imported. A workout you do not import is never uploaded.

A workout you do import is uploaded to our database — its type, times, distance, elevation, energy burned and route — because that is what lets the route be drawn on the trip's map and replayed alongside your photos.

We never use health or fitness data for advertising or marketing, and we never sell it or share it with data brokers. It is not disclosed to anyone except the infrastructure providers listed below that store it on our behalf, the map provider that draws a route in a rendered film, and the people who can see the trip it belongs to.

If you import from Strava instead, Strava may additionally supply per-second streams such as heart rate, cadence and power alongside the route, and those are stored with the activity. The paragraph above applies to them in exactly the same way.

How photographs are analysed and films are made

Some of what Rewind does with your photographs is done by machine, and it is worth being exact about which machine.

  • Finding the moments. Smaller copies of the photographs and frames of the videos you add to a trip are sent to Google's Gemini models, which score each one, say what kind of picture it is, pick the best moments, and write the outline a film is cut to. The result is stored with the trip; the copies are not kept by Google.
  • Composing a score. When you ask for music written for a film, a written description of the trip — its places, days and mood, no photographs — is sent to Google's Lyria model, which returns a piece of music we store with the film.
  • Speech in your videos. To find things people say that are worth keeping, and to time lyrics to a song, speech is transcribed on our own render servers. It is not sent to any other company.
  • Lyrics and songs. When you name a song for a film, your phone asks Deezer for its title, artwork and a thirty-second preview, and asks LRCLIB, a community lyrics catalogue, for its words. Both receive the title and artist you typed and nothing about you. The preview is used only to identify the song and measure its tempo; it is never part of a film.
  • Maps in films. The maps that routes are drawn on in a rendered film come from Mapbox, which receives the area to draw. When a route is snapped to roads, the coordinates of that route are sent to Mapbox for the purpose.

None of this makes a decision about you. Nothing you add is used to train our models or, under the terms we use these services on, theirs.

What we don't do

  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No analytics or product-measurement SDK.
  • No tracking you across other companies' apps or websites.
  • No selling your personal information, and no "sharing" of it for cross-context behavioural advertising as California law defines those terms.
  • No profiling or automated decisions that have a legal effect on you.

There is crash reporting, through Sentry: when the app crashes, the stack trace, the model of your phone, its iOS version and the app's version are sent so the fault can be found. No account identity, no photographs, and no location go with it.

What other people can see

You decide, three times over: your profile, each trip and each collection is private, friends only or public.

  • Private is the default for trips. A private trip is seen by its members: everyone you add can see every photo, video and activity in it, whoever added it, along with the name and picture of whoever did. You cannot hide one item from some members and not others — visibility is the whole trip.
  • Friends only opens a trip, a collection or your profile to the people you have accepted as friends.
  • Public opens it to anyone with the app. A public profile can be found by username, its trips browsed on a map of everywhere you have been, and followed, so that person is told when you make a new trip public. Nothing public is indexed by search engines; it is visible inside Rewind.

A trip's owner can also share a link to it. Whoever holds the link can see the trip, read-only, until the link expires — thirty days by default — or the owner revokes it. The owner can see who has opened it.

Someone you have blocked does not see your profile, your trips, your collections or your world, and you do not see theirs. A trip you are both already members of is not changed by a block; leave it, or ask its owner to remove them, if you want it to be. Blocking also ends any friendship or follow between you.

Legal bases

If you are in the UK or the European Economic Area, we rely on these grounds under the UK GDPR and GDPR:

  • Performance of a contract — for your account, your trips, your uploads, the films we make from them, and your subscription. Without these we cannot provide the app.
  • Consent — for your photo library, your device location, Apple Health, notifications, and connecting Strava. Each is a separate permission you grant, and each can be withdrawn in iOS Settings or in the app at any time. Health data is special category data and consent is the only basis we rely on for it.
  • Legitimate interests — for keeping the service secure and preventing abuse, for acting on reports, for finding and fixing faults, and for keeping records of purchases.
  • Legal obligation — where we must keep something, such as tax records of a purchase.

Withdrawing a permission does not delete what was already imported under it. You can delete that separately, or delete the whole account.

How long we keep it

  • Your trips, media, films and activities — until you delete them, or until you delete your account. Deleting a trip deletes everything in it.
  • Your account — until you delete it. We do not expire accounts for inactivity.
  • A deleted account — removed from our live systems at once, and from the storage its files were in within a day. It may persist in encrypted backups for up to a further 30 days, after which those backups expire.
  • Share links — until they expire or you revoke them. A revoked link stops working at once.
  • Crash reports — 90 days, at Sentry.
  • Reports you send about someone — until they have been acted on, and then as a record that they were.
  • Purchase records — kept as long as tax and accounting law requires, typically six or seven years, even after the account is gone. These are records of a transaction, not trip content.

Your rights

Depending on where you live you may have the right to access your data, correct it, delete it, export it, restrict or object to how we use it, and withdraw a consent. You also have the right not to be discriminated against for exercising any of them — Rewind works the same either way.

Much of this you can do yourself in the app: edit your profile, delete a photo or a trip, disconnect Strava, block someone, turn any permission off in iOS Settings, and delete the whole account from the Profile screen. For anything else, email support@example.com and we will respond within 30 days.

We do not charge for this, and we will not ask you for more identifying information than we need to be sure the request is yours.

If you are in the UK or EEA and think we have got this wrong, you can complain to your national data protection authority — in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk. We would rather you told us first so we can fix it.

Deleting your account

Open the Profile tab, scroll to the bottom, and tap Delete account. It asks twice, because there is no way back. It then removes your profile, every trip you own, everything you added to other people's trips, the activities you imported, your films, your friendships and follows, and your notification tokens, and signs you out. Your media cannot be recovered afterwards.

If you can no longer sign in, email support@example.com from the address on your account with the subject "Delete my Rewind account" and we will do it for you within 30 days, usually within a few working days, after confirming the request is yours. We will never ask for your password.

Deleting your Rewind account does not cancel an active subscription — Apple owns that relationship. Cancel it in Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions on your iPhone, and do it before you delete the account. The app reminds you of this when you have a paid plan.

Security

Your media sits in private storage — not a public bucket — and is reached through short-lived links minted for you at the moment you view it. Access is enforced by the database on every request, row by row, so being a member of one trip does not get you into another, and a trip you made public is public only to the extent you set. Traffic is encrypted in transit, and storage is encrypted at rest. Sign-in tokens are held in the iOS keychain.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your data and the law requires us to tell you, we will, without undue delay.
